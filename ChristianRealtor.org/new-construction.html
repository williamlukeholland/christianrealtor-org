Buying New?
Bring Your Own Advocate.

The builder's sales team is excellent at their job. Their job is to represent the builder. Luke's job is to represent you, before you sign a single page, through every walk-through, and long after closing day.

Two Different Roles

The Builder Has a Sales Rep.
You Should Have an Advocate.

Builder sales representatives are genuinely good at what they do. They know the floor plans, the upgrade packages, the lot premiums, and the construction timeline. They can walk you through every model in the community and answer questions with real expertise.

What they cannot do is represent your interests. They work for the builder. Their compensation depends on the sale. That is not a criticism, it is simply the structure, and it means you are navigating a significant financial decision without anyone in your corner unless you bring someone.

Luke's role is different entirely. He helps you evaluate whether the community fits your long-term goals, understand what the contract actually says before you sign it, coordinate inspections that the builder's walk-through will not replace, and think through resale value before you fall in love with an upgrade package.

On most new construction purchases, that representation costs you nothing extra. The builder structures their pricing to accommodate buyer agents. What changes is that you stop navigating alone.

The Builder's Sales Rep

Expert on the builder's product, pricing, and community. Represents the builder's interests. A valuable resource for product knowledge, but not the same as having your own advocate.

Luke, Your Buyer's Advocate

Represents your interests only. Compares builders, evaluates communities, reviews contracts, coordinates independent inspections, and gives you honest guidance on resale factors the builder's team won't raise.

Why Both Can Coexist

This is not a conflict. The builder wants a smooth, successful transaction. Luke wants you to make the right decision for your family. Those goals align far more often than they clash.

From First Visit to Move-In

Where Luke Adds Value
at Each Stage

New construction has its own rhythm, language, and pressure points. Here is where having independent guidance makes the most difference.

1

Choosing the Right Builder

Not all builders are equal in quality, customer experience, or warranty reliability. Luke helps you evaluate builders from real market experience, not marketing materials.

2

Comparing Communities

School zones, HOA structures, CDD fees, future development plans, and resale demand all vary significantly from one community to the next, often within the same city.

3

Selecting a Lot

Conservation views, corner positions, proximity to amenities or traffic, and drainage patterns affect both your daily life and your future resale value. Luke knows what to look for on a site visit.

4

Understanding Upgrades

Some upgrades add real value at resale. Others are pure builder margin. The design center is exciting and consequential. Luke helps you prioritize before you spend.

5

Reviewing Builder Contracts

Builder contracts are written by builder attorneys and tend to favor the builder. Luke reviews these with you so you understand every clause before you sign.

6

Coordinating Inspections

An independent inspection on a new home is not optional, it is essential. Luke works with trusted inspectors who specialize in new construction, including pre-drywall when timing allows.

7

The Final Walk-Through

Your last chance to document incomplete items before you own the home. Luke attends with you, knows exactly what to look for, and makes sure the punch list is complete before closing.

8

Life After Closing

Warranty questions, contractor referrals, market updates a year down the road. Luke does not disappear at the closing table. The relationship continues as long as you need it.

Common Questions

What Buyers Ask Before
Visiting Their First Model Home

Should I bring my Realtor on my first visit to a builder model home?

+

Yes, and this matters more than most buyers realize. Many builders require that a Realtor be registered on your very first visit to the community in order to represent you through the purchase. If you tour a model home without your agent and later decide to buy, the builder's sales rep may become your only point of guidance through the contract process. Coming in with your own advocate from the start costs you nothing and gives you significantly more protection.

Does having my own Realtor cost me more when buying new construction?

+

In most cases, no. Builder compensation structures vary, but many builders include buyer agent compensation as part of their standard sales process. Luke can clarify exactly how a specific builder handles this before you make any commitments. The guidance, contract review, and advocacy you receive come at no additional cost to you in most new construction transactions.

Can I still use the builder's lender when buying new construction?

+

Yes. Builders cannot legally require you to use their preferred lender, though some offer upgrade incentives for doing so. Those incentives are worth evaluating carefully alongside the actual loan terms. Luke works alongside Luis Peguero at Fairway Independent Mortgage, who can provide a side-by-side comparison so you can make the choice that actually serves you over the life of your loan, not just at the closing table.

Do I still need a home inspection on new construction?

+

Absolutely. Builder warranties cover many defects, but independent inspections catch issues that builder walk-throughs routinely miss. Framing, roofing, plumbing, and HVAC problems are far easier and less expensive to address before you close than after you own the home. A new house is not automatically a perfect house. Luke coordinates with trusted inspectors who specialize in new construction and know exactly where to look.

How long does new construction typically take from contract to closing?

+

Timelines vary depending on the builder, the community, and how far along construction is when you sign. Production builders in active Gulf Coast communities typically target six to twelve months for a build-to-order home, though permitting, supply chain conditions, and build-out pace all affect this. Some communities offer quick-move-in homes already under construction or complete. Luke helps you understand what to realistically expect before you commit to a timeline that affects your current lease or existing home sale.

Can I negotiate upgrades or the purchase price on a new construction home?

+

This depends heavily on the builder, the community, and the current pace of sales. Some builders have firm pricing and offer incentives through financing or design center credits rather than price reductions. Others have more flexibility, particularly on inventory homes that have been sitting for a few months. Luke knows the local market and can help you understand what leverage exists in a given situation before you begin any conversation with the builder's team.

An Honest Assessment

Why New Construction Works
for the Right Buyer

There are real and meaningful reasons to choose new over resale. There are also situations where resale makes far more sense. Here is an honest look at both sides.

Modern Floor Plans

Open-concept layouts, primary suites designed for today's standards, dedicated office space, and flexible rooms that adapt to how families actually live. Resale homes from even ten years ago often cannot match this without significant renovation.

Energy Efficiency

Current Florida building codes require insulation, windows, and HVAC systems that significantly outperform older homes. Meritage's spray foam standard and similar approaches from other builders translate to meaningfully lower utility bills month over month.

Builder Warranties

Florida law requires a one-year workmanship warranty, a two-year systems warranty, and a ten-year structural warranty on new construction. This protection does not exist when buying a used home, and it matters more than most buyers realize in the first few years of ownership.

Personalization

Depending on where a home sits in the construction cycle, buyers may be able to choose finishes, cabinetry, flooring, and structural options through the design center. That experience, whatever its limitations, produces a home that feels genuinely theirs from day one.

Lower Near-Term Maintenance

New roofs, new appliances, new plumbing and electrical systems mean fewer unexpected costs in the early years. For budget-conscious buyers or those who simply want predictability, this is a meaningful advantage over taking on an older home's unknowns.

Community Amenities

Master-planned communities on the Gulf Coast routinely include resort pools, fitness centers, pickleball courts, trails, and event programming built into the community from the start. That lifestyle infrastructure is difficult to replicate in an established neighborhood.

A word about resale: New construction is not the right choice for every buyer. If you need a specific location that lacks new development, if you want established trees and a neighborhood with history, if your timeline requires a fast close, or if your budget favors value over newness, a resale home may be the smarter path. Luke represents buyers in both situations and will give you an honest recommendation based on your goals, not which transaction is more convenient for him.
