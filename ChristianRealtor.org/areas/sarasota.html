Discover Sarasota

More Than One Kind of Coastal Living

Sarasota is a city on Sarasota Bay, with barrier islands and the Gulf beaches just across the water. Its homes range from condominium towers downtown and on the bayfront to historic bungalows, midcentury neighborhoods, and estates on the bay and the canals.

Two phrases come up constantly. "West of Trail" refers to the established neighborhoods between the Tamiami Trail (US-41) and the bay, prized for their proximity to the water and downtown. "The keys" refers to the barrier islands, such as Bird Key, St. Armands, Lido, and Siesta, each with its own character and coastal considerations.

Sarasota is also a market where coastal details carry real weight: flood zones, building age, condominium finances, and insurance can matter as much as the view.

Explore the Neighborhoods

Six Neighborhoods, Six Different Sarasotas

Downtown · Condos

Downtown Sarasota & Golden Gate Point

Condominium living within walking distance of restaurants, galleries, and the bayfront, including the condo towers on Golden Gate Point, a small peninsula off downtown. Best for buyers who value walkability and low exterior maintenance.

Historic

Laurel Park

A small historic district just southeast of downtown, listed on the National Register of Historic Places, with bungalows, mature trees, and narrow streets. Proof that Sarasota's appeal goes well beyond waterfront luxury.

West of Trail

Harbor Acres & West of Trail

Established streets between US-41 and Sarasota Bay, with distinctive homes and bayfront or canal settings in select locations. Harbor Acres is among the area's best-known enclaves for higher-end buyers.

North Sarasota

Indian Beach & Sapphire Shores

Bayside neighborhoods north of downtown with architectural variety, mature landscaping, and close proximity to The Ringling museum and the area's cultural landmarks.

Islands

Bird Key & St. Armands

Island neighborhoods between downtown and the Gulf, with many homes on the water and quick access to St. Armands Circle and Lido Beach. Water access and dock depth vary by lot, so verify both for any home.

Midcentury

Southgate & Arlington Park

Established neighborhoods with midcentury homes, many renovated, on traditional residential streets. A practical, popular contrast to Sarasota's coastal condo market.

See what's for sale in any of these neighborhoods on the Search Homes page. Elsewhere on the Gulf Coast, compare Lakewood Ranch & Parrish or St. Petersburg.

Waterfront & Coastal Buying

The View Is Only Part of the Story

A home on Sarasota's coastline can offer an extraordinary setting, but the details matter. From water access and condominium requirements to insurance, building condition, and long-term costs, these are the questions to answer before you commit.

Comparing Sarasota with St. Petersburg, the Pinellas beaches, or Apollo Beach? See our Tampa Bay & Gulf Coast waterfront homes guide.

  1. Water access. Bayfront, canal-front, Gulf-front, and water-view homes are not interchangeable. Confirm whether a home has a dock, how deep the water is, and the route to open water.
  2. Condominium due diligence. Review the association's budget, reserves, milestone inspection and reserve study where required, recent and planned assessments, and building maintenance. Our printable condo due-diligence checklist keeps it organized.
  3. Flood zone and elevation. Many coastal homes are in FEMA high-risk flood zones. Ask for the elevation certificate and any flood or storm damage history.
  4. Insurance and wind exposure. Get flood and wind quotes during your inspection period. Costs vary greatly with elevation, age, and construction.
  5. Seawalls, docks, and restrictions. Have a marine contractor inspect seawalls and docks, and check permits and any association rules on boats and lifts.
  6. Property fit. Weigh privacy, walkability, boating access, maintenance, and how often you'll be there. Seasonal owners have different priorities.

Local Sarasota

Gardens, Islands, and the Bayfront

Marie Selby Botanical Gardens

A bayfront botanical garden in downtown Sarasota, known for its tropical plant collections, art exhibitions, and views across Sarasota Bay. Plan a visit.

St. Armands Circle

A historic circle of shops and restaurants on St. Armands Key, with a park at its center and Lido Beach a short walk away. Visitor information.

The Bay Park

The city's newest signature waterfront park, on Sarasota Bay near the Boulevard of the Arts, with walking paths, a sunset deck, and free community events. Events and park map.

Distinctive Homes Deserve a More Considered Approach.

Location is only the beginning. Architecture, renovations, water access, condominium details, and competing properties all influence how a Sarasota home should be positioned.

A thoughtful selling strategy starts with understanding what makes your home different, then pricing it against the properties buyers will actually compare it to.

Sarasota Questions

Sarasota Real Estate: Common Questions

Downtown Sarasota is mostly condominium living: towers and mid-rise buildings within walking distance of restaurants, arts venues, and the bayfront, with exterior maintenance handled by the association. The established neighborhoods around it, such as Laurel Park, the West of Trail areas, and Southgate, are mostly single-family homes, from historic bungalows to midcentury ranches and larger homes on the bay, where you own and maintain the house and lot yourself. Buyers usually choose between walkability and low maintenance on one hand, and space and privacy on the other.

Waterfront homes are found along Sarasota Bay in neighborhoods such as Harbor Acres and others West of Trail, on canals and the bay in island communities such as Bird Key, along the bayfront north of downtown, and in condominium buildings downtown and on Golden Gate Point. Gulf-front homes and condos are on the barrier islands. Bayfront, canal-front, Gulf-front, and water-view properties differ greatly in boating access, exposure, and cost, so confirm dock depth, route to open water, and the flood zone for each property.

Florida now requires condominium buildings three or more stories tall to complete periodic milestone structural inspections and to fund reserves for key building components based on a structural integrity reserve study. For a buyer, that makes the association's financial health as important as the unit itself. Before your review period ends, ask for the budget, reserve study, most recent milestone inspection where required, recent and planned special assessments, insurance coverage, and board meeting minutes.

"West of Trail" refers to the area between the Tamiami Trail (US-41) and Sarasota Bay, generally south of downtown. It includes some of Sarasota's most established and sought-after neighborhoods, such as Harbor Acres, prized for their proximity to the bay, downtown, and the keys. Homes range from original midcentury houses to newer custom homes, and bayfront lots command a significant premium.

Decide how you want to live day to day: walkable downtown, a quiet established neighborhood, or a home on the water or the islands. Check whether an address is inside the City of Sarasota or unincorporated Sarasota County, since services and some rules differ. Price insurance early, because coastal location and elevation have a large effect on cost, and look up the flood zone on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Hurricanes Helene and Milton in 2024 were a reminder that storm exposure matters along this coast. Sarasota–Bradenton International Airport and I-75 make travel straightforward, and Lakewood Ranch offers an inland, master-planned alternative nearby.

Value starts with comparable sales, but for distinctive properties the right comparables are often few and far between. Adjustments for water access and frontage, view, elevation and flood history, lot size, architecture, age and quality of renovations, and, for condos, the building's condition and finances all play a larger role than in a typical neighborhood.
