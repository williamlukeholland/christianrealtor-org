FishHawk & Lithia Real Estate

Southeast of Brandon in Hillsborough County, FishHawk and Lithia combine shaded streets, miles of trails, and conservation land with established neighborhoods people seek out on purpose. Whether you want a home in a master-planned community or a few acres of your own, this is where to start.

An Introduction

Two Ways to Live in Lithia

FishHawk Ranch is the area's signature master-planned community: neighborhoods built around parks, pools, a network of walking and biking trails, and a town center with shops and restaurants. Homes range from townhomes to large family homes, and community districts maintain the landscaping, trails, and amenity areas.

Outside the planned communities, greater Lithia is more rural. Buyers find homes on larger lots, acreage with room for horses or workshops, and custom-built houses on quiet roads. The trade-off is fewer shared amenities, and often a private well and septic system to maintain.

What both share is the setting: mature oaks, the Alafia River nearby, and county parks and preserves within a short drive. It's also an established area, which is why many people living here today are deciding what their next move should be.

Four Distinct Ways to Call It Home

These names get used loosely, but they aren't interchangeable. Each has its own amenities and, in some cases, its own HOA and community development district, so fees and rules can differ even a few streets apart.

FishHawk Ranch

The original master-planned community, with established neighborhoods, mature landscaping, and a trail network connecting parks, pools, and recreation areas. FishHawk Ranch Towncenter adds shops and dining within the community.

Best for: buyers who want an established neighborhood with amenities already in place.

FishHawk Ranch West

A separate community with its own homeowners association and district. Its LakeHouse amenity center includes pools, a fitness center, a game room, and dog parks, and it's a distinct place to live rather than simply another section of FishHawk Ranch.

Good to know: its HOA and fees are separate from FishHawk Ranch, so compare both before you choose.

Starling at FishHawk

One of the newer parts of FishHawk, with its own amenity center, pool, fitness facilities, and gathering spaces. Homes here are generally newer than in the original neighborhoods.

Best for: buyers who want a newer home with the FishHawk location. Confirm which HOA and district apply to the specific address.

Greater Lithia & Acreage Homes

Beyond the planned communities are homes on larger lots, acreage properties, and custom homes with more privacy and fewer neighbors. Some allow horses, barns, or workshops that most HOAs would not.

Good to know: check zoning, deed restrictions, well and septic condition, and flood zones near the Alafia River and creeks.

The Lifestyle

Trails, Springs, and Saturday Games

Lithia Springs Park

A Hillsborough County park built around a natural spring on the Alafia River, with shaded trails, a campground, and a swimming area that's been a local favorite for generations. Swimming depends on water conditions, so check the county's park page before you go.

Fish Hawk Creek Preserve

Protected county land with hiking trails, birdwatching, and a quieter, wilder side of the area's outdoors. Trails can close after heavy rain or for prescribed burns, so check trail status first.

FishHawk Sports Complex

The county sports complex in FishHawk hosts youth soccer, baseball, and football leagues, and on weekends it's the center of the area's family life. Leagues are run by county partner organizations.

Inside FishHawk Ranch itself, neighborhood parks, pools, and the trail network are maintained by the community's districts and associations.

Questions Buyers Actually Ask

FishHawk & Lithia: Common Questions

They are neighboring master-planned communities in Lithia, but they are governed separately. FishHawk Ranch West has its own homeowners association and its own community development district, with amenities centered on its LakeHouse. FishHawk Ranch has its own district and associations, its trail network, and the FishHawk Ranch Towncenter. Because the HOAs and districts are separate, fees, rules, and amenity access differ, so compare the specific address rather than assuming everything with the FishHawk name works the same way.

Most homes in the FishHawk communities pay both. HOA dues cover the association's amenities and common areas, while community development district (CDD) assessments appear on the annual property tax bill and fund infrastructure and district-maintained parks, trails, and landscaping. Amounts vary by community, neighborhood, and even lot, so the only reliable number is the one for the specific address. Before making an offer, look up the property's tax bill on the Hillsborough County Tax Collector's website and ask for the HOA's current dues and budget. Luke reviews both with every buyer.

Insurance can also cost more for larger or older homes, so get a quote during your inspection period.

Most FishHawk and Lithia commuters head north through Brandon or west toward I-75, using roads such as FishHawk Boulevard, Boyette Road, Bloomingdale Avenue, and Lithia Pinecrest Road. From I-75, the Selmon Expressway is a common route into downtown Tampa. Drive times depend heavily on the time of day, so test the commute at the hours you'll actually drive before choosing a neighborhood.

FishHawk and Lithia are served by Hillsborough County Public Schools. School boundaries can change, and neighboring streets are sometimes assigned to different schools, so don't rely on a listing description or a neighborhood name. Use the school district's official school boundary locator for the exact address, and contact the district directly if the address is near a boundary or in a newer section.

FishHawk and Lithia are inland and not exposed to coastal storm surge, but rivers, creeks, wetlands, and retention ponds run throughout the area, and the Alafia River can flood after heavy rain. Some homes are in FEMA flood zones and some are not. Look up the address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, ask the seller about any past water intrusion, and price a flood policy even if one isn't required.
