Two Distinct Lifestyles. One Place to Call Home.

Riverview & Apollo Beach Real Estate

From Riverview's established neighborhoods and growing communities to Apollo Beach's canals and waterfront homes, the southern stretch of Hillsborough County along Tampa Bay offers two very different ways to live. Both are a short drive from Tampa via I-75 and US-301.

Two Ways to Live South of Tampa

Riverview or Apollo Beach? What Really Differs

Both are unincorporated communities in Hillsborough County, served by the same county government and school district. The real differences are the homes, the water, and what you'll pay to insure them.

Hillsborough County · ZIPs 33578, 33579, 33569

Riverview

A large, mostly inland community named for the Alafia River, which runs through its northern end. Neighborhoods range from established subdivisions to master-planned communities still adding homes.

  • You'll find: single-family homes and townhomes across a wide range of ages and price points, with amenities in most planned communities.
  • Trade-offs: CDD and HOA fees are common, and traffic on US-301, Big Bend Road, and the I-75 interchanges has grown with the population.
  • Water: mostly inland, with river and creek frontage in some older areas.
Hillsborough County · ZIP 33572

Apollo Beach

A bayfront community where much of the original neighborhood was built along dredged canals leading to Tampa Bay, alongside newer master-planned communities to the east.

  • You'll find: canal-front homes with docks, gated waterfront communities, and newer construction in larger planned communities.
  • Trade-offs: higher flood and wind insurance near the water, more storm exposure, and seawalls and docks to maintain.
  • Water: some homes have direct boat access to Tampa Bay; many do not, even within the same community.

Six Communities Worth Knowing

Riverview

Winthrop

A walkable, new-urbanist neighborhood near Bloomingdale Avenue, with front-porch homes, oak-shaded streets, a village green, and its own town centre with dining and events. A very different feel from typical suburban subdivisions.

Riverview

Panther Trace

An established master-planned community with landscaped streets, preserved wetlands, and recreation amenities. Homes are mostly from the 2000s, with a mix of sizes and price points.

Riverview

South Fork

A southern Riverview community near US-301 and Big Bend Road with single-family homes, community amenities, and quick access south toward Apollo Beach and east toward I-75.

Apollo Beach

MiraBay

A waterfront-oriented community with canals, coastal-inspired architecture, and a central clubhouse. Many homes are on the water, and some have boat access to Tampa Bay; confirm it lot by lot.

Apollo Beach

Waterset

A large master-planned community with several builders, multiple home styles, trails, and extensive amenity centers. Much of it is newer construction, and most homes are not on Tampa Bay itself.

Apollo Beach

Andalucia

A smaller, gated community with Mediterranean-style homes and a more upscale feel. Some homes sit on the water, so check whether a specific lot has boat access before you assume it does.

Waterfront Buyers

What to Know Before Buying Waterfront in Apollo Beach

"Waterfront" can mean a canal with a dock and a straight run to Tampa Bay, or a pond view with no boat access at all. These are the questions worth answering before you write an offer. To compare Apollo Beach with waterfront options in South Tampa, St. Petersburg, and Sarasota, see our Tampa Bay waterfront homes guide.

  1. Seawall condition. Repairs and replacements are expensive. Have a marine contractor inspect the seawall, not just the home inspector.
  2. Docks, lifts, and permits. Ask for permits for the existing dock and boat lift. New or rebuilt docks require permits, and the rules affect what you can build.
  3. Canal depth and access. Some canals are shallow at low tide. Confirm the route and water depth for the boat you plan to own.
  4. Bridges and clearance. Bridges on some routes limit sailboat masts and taller boats. Check clearance before you buy.
  5. Flood zone and elevation. Many waterfront homes are in high-risk flood zones. Ask for the elevation certificate and any flood claims history.
  6. Insurance and storm exposure. Get flood and wind quotes during your inspection period. Waterfront homes can cost several times more to insure than inland homes nearby.

What Buyers Should Know

Riverview & Apollo Beach: Common Questions

Both are unincorporated communities in Hillsborough County, so they share the same county government and public school district. Riverview is larger and mostly inland, with a wide range of established subdivisions and master-planned communities. Apollo Beach sits on Tampa Bay, with canal-front homes, gated waterfront communities, and newer planned communities to the east. In practice, the biggest differences are the type of home, access to the water, and the cost of insuring a home near the bay.

Yes, but not every waterfront home has it. Many homes along Apollo Beach's canals have docks with a route to Tampa Bay, while other homes face ponds, lakes, or preserves with no boat access. Even where there is access, canal depth, the route to open water, and bridge clearance can limit which boats will work. Confirm the access, depth, and dock permits for the specific property before you make an offer.

Many Apollo Beach homes near the bay and canals are in FEMA special flood hazard areas, where lenders require flood insurance, and coastal homes face storm surge during hurricanes. Premiums depend heavily on the home's elevation and flood zone, so two similar homes on the same street can cost very different amounts to insure. Look up the address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, ask for the elevation certificate and flood claims history, and get flood and wind quotes during your inspection period.

Most master-planned communities in Riverview have an HOA, and many also have a community development district (CDD). HOA dues cover amenities and common areas, while CDD assessments appear on the annual property tax bill. Amounts vary widely by community and even by section, so check the actual tax bill for the address on the Hillsborough County Tax Collector's website and ask for the HOA's current dues before you make an offer.

New construction brings modern layouts, builder warranties, and energy-efficient systems, but often comes with higher CDD assessments, smaller or newer landscaping, and less room to negotiate on price. Resale homes are usually in established neighborhoods with mature trees and known fee histories, and they may leave more room for negotiation. If you visit a builder's model, bring your agent on the first visit; many builders won't let an agent represent you otherwise.

Most commuters use I-75 or US-301 north toward Brandon and Tampa, with the Selmon Expressway a common route into downtown. Big Bend Road and US-301 carry much of the local traffic and can be congested at rush hour. Drive times depend heavily on time of day, so test your commute at the hours you'll actually drive.
