St. Petersburg, FL Homes for Sale & Real Estate

St. Petersburg sits on the Pinellas peninsula with Tampa Bay on one side and the Gulf beaches a short drive away. Buyers come for a walkable downtown on the water, historic neighborhoods with brick streets and bungalows, and canal-front homes with boat access. Living in St. Petersburg

What Buyers Should Know First

A Downtown Built Around the Water

Downtown St. Pete faces Tampa Bay, with waterfront parks, the St. Pete Pier, marinas, and museums along Beach Drive and Central Avenue. Most downtown housing is condos and townhomes, so a building's age, reserves, and insurance matter as much as the unit. Our waterfront condo buyer's guide explains what to request.

Historic Homes, Real Character

Neighborhoods like Old Northeast and Kenwood are filled with 1910s through 1930s bungalows, Mission and Mediterranean Revival homes, and brick streets. Older homes bring charm along with things to inspect closely: roofs, electrical, plumbing, and foundations.

Water Access and Getting Around

Canal and bayfront homes in areas like Snell Isle and Shore Acres offer docks and boat access to Tampa Bay. Commuters to Tampa cross the Howard Frankland or Gandy Bridge, and both Tampa International and St. Pete–Clearwater International airports are within reach.

Explore Neighborhoods

St. Petersburg, Neighborhood by Neighborhood

Downtown St. Petersburg

High-rise and mid-rise condos, townhomes, and a few historic single-family streets, all within walking distance of the waterfront, restaurants, museums, and the Pier. Best for buyers who want to park the car for the weekend.

Historic Old Northeast

Just north of downtown, with brick streets, sidewalks, and early 1900s homes, many with garage apartments. It runs toward the waterfront parks along North Shore Drive. Ask about historic designations before planning exterior changes.

Snell Isle

A 1920s island neighborhood northeast of downtown, known for Mediterranean Revival estates, waterfront lots on the bayous, and a golf course. Some of the city's most valuable homes are here, along with real flood exposure.

Kenwood

West of downtown near the Grand Central District, Kenwood is known for its many 1920s Craftsman bungalows, an active neighborhood association, and an arts-minded community. It sits inland, away from the waterfront.

Shore Acres

A northeast St. Pete neighborhood on Tampa Bay laced with canals, where many homes have docks. Mostly mid-century ranch homes, with a growing number of elevated rebuilds after recent storm surge flooding.

St. Petersburg Questions

Buying & Selling in St. Pete: Common Questions

St. Petersburg has a walkable, waterfront downtown with parks, museums, restaurants, and the St. Pete Pier, surrounded by older residential neighborhoods with their own character. Gulf beaches such as St. Pete Beach and Pass-a-Grille are a short drive west. Pinellas County is densely developed, so new single-family construction usually means rebuilding on an existing lot rather than a new master-planned community. Commuters to Tampa cross Tampa Bay by bridge, and travel time depends heavily on traffic.

Neighborhoods like Old Northeast and Kenwood have many homes built in the 1910s through 1930s. Plan for a thorough home inspection plus the 4-point inspection (roof, electrical, plumbing, HVAC) that many Florida insurers require on older homes, and ask about past updates to wiring, plumbing, and the roof. Some streets and properties carry local or national historic designations, which can affect exterior changes, so confirm with the City of St. Petersburg before you plan a renovation.

Many St. Pete neighborhoods near Tampa Bay, the bayous, and the canals are in FEMA special flood hazard areas, where lenders require flood insurance. Storm surge from Hurricane Helene in 2024 flooded homes in several waterfront neighborhoods, including Shore Acres and Snell Isle. Before you commit, look up the flood zone on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, ask for the elevation certificate and flood history, and get a flood insurance quote during your inspection period. If you plan major renovations, ask about the FEMA 50% rule, which can require an older home to be elevated.

Florida now requires older condo buildings of three or more stories to complete milestone structural inspections and to fully fund reserves for key components such as roofs and structural elements. For buyers, that makes the association's financials as important as the unit. Ask for the most recent milestone inspection, the structural integrity reserve study, the current budget and reserves, any pending special assessments, and the building's insurance coverage before your review period ends.

Since October 2024, Florida sellers of residential property must give buyers a flood disclosure that covers whether they have filed a flood insurance claim and whether they received federal flood assistance for the home. Many St. Pete buyers will also ask for the elevation certificate and any repair records. Having these ready before listing helps avoid delays once you're under contract.
