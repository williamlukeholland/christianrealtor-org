Sun City Center FL Real Estate

Low-maintenance villas, single-family homes on golf courses, and neighborhoods with clubs and activities close to home: Sun City Center offers a distinctive approach to Florida living. It's also a place where the details matter, because communities here come with different associations, rules, and fees.

Life in Sun City Center

A Community Built Around Choice

Sun City Center was founded in the early 1960s as one of Florida's first planned active-adult communities, and its core neighborhoods remain age-restricted to households with at least one resident 55 or older. Over the decades it has grown into a collection of neighborhoods and associations, each with its own homes, amenities, and rules.

Daily life here can be as full or as quiet as you like. Residents have access to pools, fitness centers, golf, and a long list of clubs through their community associations, and Sun City Center is one of the few places in Florida where golf carts are a common way to get around on designated roads.

Homes range from attached villas and condominiums with exterior maintenance included to single-family homes you maintain yourself. Understanding that difference, and which association a home belongs to, is the most important step in choosing well.

Four Different Ways to Live Here

The name "Sun City Center" covers several communities that are governed separately. Ownership type, maintenance, fees, and amenity access can differ a great deal between them.

Gated · 55+

Kings Point

A gated, age-restricted community made up mostly of attached villas and condominiums, with large clubhouses, pools, golf, and more than a hundred clubs. Kings Point has its own governing structure, separate from the Sun City Center Community Association, and each building or section belongs to its own condominium association.

Good to know: condo fees often cover exterior maintenance and some insurance, but what's included varies by association. Read the specific association's documents.

55+

Sun City Center Community Association Neighborhoods

The original, established neighborhoods of Sun City Center, with single-family homes, villas, mature landscaping, and access to the Community Association's recreation facilities and clubs, subject to its membership rules and dues.

Good to know: single-family owners usually handle their own roof, exterior, and insurance, on top of Community Association dues and any neighborhood HOA.

Golf

Renaissance

A more upscale residential option associated with golf-course surroundings and distinctive single-family homes, for buyers who want a more elevated setting within Sun City Center.

Good to know: golf membership is typically separate from the home purchase. Confirm the association, age rules, and club costs.

Gated

Verona

A gated enclave of single-family homes that are generally newer than those in Sun City Center's older sections, appealing to buyers who want newer construction and a more private setting.

Good to know: confirm whether the specific home is age-restricted and which association and fees apply.

Finding the Right Fit

How to Compare Communities Here

Two homes at the same price can come with very different monthly costs and responsibilities. These are the questions that separate them.

  1. Ownership type. Is it a condominium, a villa in an HOA, or a single-family home? That determines what you own and what the association owns.
  2. All of the fees. Add up condo or HOA fees, Community Association dues, and any special assessments. Ask what each one covers.
  3. Maintenance and insurance. Find out who is responsible for the roof, exterior, landscaping, and insurance on the building.
  4. Amenity access. Confirm which pools, clubhouses, and clubs come with the home, and whether golf is a separate membership.
  5. Age and occupancy rules. Read the association's age-verification, guest, and rental policies before you commit.
  6. Association health. Review budgets, reserves, recent assessments, and meeting minutes, especially in condominium communities.

Selling and Buying at the Same Time?

  • Sequence. Sell first, buy first, or close both on the same day. Each has trade-offs.
  • Financing. Your lender can tell you whether you can buy before your current home sells.

Sun City Center: Common Questions

The core Sun City Center communities, including the Community Association neighborhoods and Kings Point, are age-restricted, generally requiring at least one resident in each home to be 55 or older under federal housing rules for older persons. Each community sets its own age-verification, guest, and occupancy policies. Not every home with a Sun City Center mailing address is in an age-restricted community, so confirm the rules for the specific address and read the association's documents before you buy.

Kings Point is a gated, age-restricted community within the Sun City Center area, but it is governed separately from the Sun City Center Community Association. It has its own bylaws, its own clubhouses and recreation, and more than a hundred individual condominium associations, and most of its homes are attached villas and condominiums. The Community Association neighborhoods include more single-family homes, and their residents use the Community Association's facilities under its membership rules. Fees, maintenance responsibilities, and amenity access differ between the two.
