Wesley Chapel & New Tampa Real Estate

From established New Tampa neighborhoods beneath mature oaks to newer Wesley Chapel communities with room to grow, these two neighbors along I-75 north of Tampa offer two distinct ways to feel at home. They sit side by side, but they're in different counties, which affects taxes, schools, and services. Here's how they compare.

Two Communities, Two Lifestyles

Wesley Chapel or New Tampa? The Honest Comparison

Buyers often search both. The right choice usually comes down to how new you want the home, how you commute, and which county you'd rather live in.

Pasco County · Unincorporated

Wesley Chapel

One of the fastest-growing parts of Tampa Bay, built largely around master-planned communities with pools, clubhouses, and trails. Shopping, hospitals, and youth sports facilities have grown along with it.

  • You'll find: newer homes, active new construction, and community amenities built in.
  • Trade-offs: CDD and HOA fees are common, and growth brings traffic on SR 56 and around the I-75 interchanges.
  • Services: Pasco County schools and county services, not a city government.
Hillsborough County · City of Tampa

New Tampa

The northeast corner of the City of Tampa, developed mostly in the 1980s through the 2000s. Neighborhoods are established, landscaping is mature, and many communities are gated, some around golf courses.

  • You'll find: resale homes with mature trees, larger established communities, and quick access to USF and I-75.
  • Trade-offs: little new construction left, older homes to inspect closely, and Bruce B. Downs Boulevard at rush hour.
  • Services: City of Tampa services and Hillsborough County schools.

Six Neighborhoods Worth Knowing

Wesley Chapel

Estancia at Wiregrass

A newer neighborhood within Wiregrass Ranch with Mediterranean-inspired architecture and a resort-style amenity center, close to The Shops at Wiregrass and the sports campus.

Wesley Chapel

The Ridge at Wiregrass Ranch

A gated section of Wiregrass Ranch with larger, newer homes. Buyers looking here usually want newer construction and privacy without leaving the center of Wesley Chapel.

Wesley Chapel

Seven Oaks

An established master-planned community off SR 56 with recreation facilities, pools, and a wide range of home sizes and price points, from starter homes to larger family homes.

New Tampa

Tampa Palms

One of New Tampa's original planned communities, made up of separate villages, many of them gated. Mature landscaping, parks, and quick access to I-75 and the USF area.

New Tampa

Hunter's Green

A gated golf community surrounded by preserved land, with a country club and sports amenities. Flatwoods Park and its trails are close by.

New Tampa

Arbor Greene

An established, gated residential community off Cross Creek Boulevard with a traditional neighborhood feel, tree-lined streets, and a central clubhouse.

Living Here

Everyday Life in North Tampa

Shopping & Dining

The Shops at Wiregrass is an outdoor shopping and events center at the heart of Wesley Chapel. Tampa Premium Outlets sits nearby on SR 56, and KRATE at The Grove brings small local shops and restaurants together in a shipping-container market.

Parks & Recreation

Flatwoods Park, near Hunter's Green, has a long paved loop popular with cyclists and runners. Families use the Wiregrass Ranch Sports Campus for youth tournaments and the New Tampa Recreation Center for classes, courts, and community programs.

Healthcare

AdventHealth Wesley Chapel and BayCare Hospital Wesley Chapel both serve the area, and New Tampa residents are close to the medical facilities around USF and the University area.

Getting Around

I-75 runs through both communities, with I-275 joining it to the north. Bruce B. Downs Boulevard is the main route between New Tampa and the University area, and SR 56 carries most Wesley Chapel traffic. Plan your commute at the times you'll actually drive it.

Buyer Questions Answered

Wesley Chapel & New Tampa: Common Questions

They sit next to each other along I-75, but they are in different counties. New Tampa is part of the City of Tampa in Hillsborough County, so homes there get City of Tampa services and Hillsborough County schools. Wesley Chapel is an unincorporated community in Pasco County, with Pasco County schools and county services. Property tax rates, school assignments, and utility providers differ as a result, so confirm all three for any specific address. In general, New Tampa is more established with mature landscaping, while Wesley Chapel has more newer homes and active new construction.

Most do. Master-planned communities in both areas usually have an HOA for amenities and common areas, and many also have a Community Development District (CDD). CDD assessments appear on the property tax bill, not as a separate HOA payment, and they vary widely from one community to the next, sometimes even between sections of the same community. Before you write an offer, check the actual tax bill for the address on the county tax collector's website and ask for the HOA budget. Luke reviews both with every buyer.

Most new construction is in Wesley Chapel and the surrounding parts of Pasco County, where large master-planned communities are still building. New Tampa is mostly built out, so homes there are primarily resale. If you plan to visit a builder's model, bring your agent on the first visit; many builders will not let an agent represent you if you registered without one. The new construction guide covers builders and the process in more detail.

Both areas rely on I-75, with I-275 joining it to the north. From New Tampa, Bruce B. Downs Boulevard is the main route to USF and the University area, and it is heavily traveled at rush hour. From Wesley Chapel, SR 56 connects most communities to I-75. Drive times to downtown Tampa, Westshore, or the airport depend heavily on time of day, so test the drive at the hours you'll actually commute before you choose a neighborhood.

These areas are inland and are not exposed to coastal storm surge the way South Tampa or St. Petersburg are, but they are not flood-free. Creeks, wetlands, conservation land, and retention ponds run throughout both, and some homes near them are in FEMA flood zones. Look up the address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, ask the seller about any past water intrusion, and get an insurance quote during your inspection period. Even outside a required flood zone, a flood policy is often inexpensive and worth pricing.
