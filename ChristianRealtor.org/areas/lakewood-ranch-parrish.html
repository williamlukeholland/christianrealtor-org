Lakewood Ranch & Parrish Real Estate

Explore the established villages of Lakewood Ranch and the growing communities of Parrish. They sit a few miles apart across the Manatee River, but they're different markets: Lakewood Ranch spans Manatee and Sarasota counties with mature village amenities, while Parrish, in Manatee County, is where much of the area's newest construction is happening.

Two Markets, Different Possibilities

Lakewood Ranch or Parrish? How They Compare

Both appeal to move-up buyers, families, and people relocating to the Gulf Coast. The differences are in how established the communities are, what amenities come with them, and which county you'll live in.

Manatee & Sarasota Counties

Lakewood Ranch

A large master-planned community east of I-75 that has been growing since the 1990s, organized into villages with their own neighborhoods, amenities, and associations, plus two town centers: Main Street and Waterside Place.

  • You'll find: established and newer villages, golf and country club living, lakefront homes, and a wide range of price points.
  • Trade-offs: HOA dues and CDD assessments in most villages, and some neighborhoods with required club memberships.
  • Check: which county the address is in; it affects taxes, schools, and services.
Manatee County · ZIP 34219

Parrish

North of the Manatee River along I-75 and US-301, Parrish has grown quickly with new master-planned communities, while still holding older homes and more rural, larger-lot properties at its edges.

  • You'll find: active new construction, newer planned communities, and some established and rural properties.
  • Trade-offs: CDD and HOA fees in most new communities, ongoing construction nearby, and growing traffic on US-301.
  • Getting around: I-75 north toward Tampa and St. Petersburg; south toward Bradenton and Sarasota.

New Construction vs. Resale

The Right Home Is About More Than the Floor Plan

A brand-new home offers modern finishes and the chance to personalize. An established home may offer mature landscaping, finished improvements, and a neighborhood you can see today. The right choice comes from understanding the full picture.

  1. Incentives vs. the real price. Builder incentives can be generous, but compare the final price, including lot premiums and options, against similar resale homes.
  2. Lot premiums and upgrades. Water views, larger lots, and design-center selections add up quickly. Decide your priorities before you sit down to choose.
  3. Completion timelines. Build dates can move. Understand what the contract says about delays and your rate lock.
  4. HOA and CDD obligations. Most villages and new communities have both. Check the actual CDD assessment for the lot or address, not an average.
  5. Independent inspections. New homes benefit from independent inspections at key stages, not just a final walkthrough.
  6. Resale competition. When you sell, you may compete with the builder's own new inventory. Location and lot help a home hold its value.

Considering a Builder's Model Home? Talk With Luke Before Your First Visit.

Builders set their own registration and agent-representation policies, and many require your agent to be with you, or registered, on your very first visit. Clarify representation before you sign in at a sales center or sign anything, because it's often too late to add an agent afterward.
