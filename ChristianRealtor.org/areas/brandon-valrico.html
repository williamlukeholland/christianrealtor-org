Brandon & Valrico Real Estate

From Brandon's convenient, well-established neighborhoods to Valrico's tree-lined streets and golf communities, explore homes in two of eastern Hillsborough County's most recognizable markets, whether you're buying your next home or getting ready to sell this one.

Brandon vs. Valrico

Next-Door Neighbors, Different Feel

Both are unincorporated communities in Hillsborough County with the same county government and school district. The differences are in the age of the neighborhoods, the lot sizes, and how close you are to the shopping and highways along SR 60.

Hillsborough County · ZIPs 33510, 33511

Brandon

One of the county's most established suburbs, with neighborhoods built over several decades around the SR 60 corridor, its shopping and dining, and Brandon's hospital and medical offices.

  • You'll find: a deep resale market of single-family homes, townhomes, and some older homes on larger lots.
  • Trade-offs: homes built across many decades mean condition varies widely, and SR 60 traffic can be heavy.
  • Getting around: quick access to I-75 and the Selmon Expressway into Tampa.
Hillsborough County · ZIPs 33594, 33596

Valrico

East of Brandon, Valrico is greener and a little more spread out, with mature oaks, larger homes in many neighborhoods, golf communities, and established subdivisions around Bloomingdale and Bell Shoals Road.

  • You'll find: established single-family neighborhoods, gated golf living, and more room per home in many areas.
  • Trade-offs: a longer drive to I-75, and HOA rules that vary widely from one subdivision to the next.
  • Nearby: borders FishHawk & Lithia to the south.

Six Neighborhoods Worth Knowing

Brandon

Providence Lakes

Established neighborhoods with lakes running through parts of the community and easy access to shopping and major roads. A steady choice for buyers who want a settled neighborhood close to everything.

Brandon

Heather Lakes

A recognizable Brandon neighborhood of traditional single-family homes on established streets, a good fit for buyers who prefer a resale home with a yard over new construction.

Brandon

Limona & Historic Brandon

Limona was settled in the 1870s, before Brandon grew up around it. Today its older pockets offer mature trees, varied architecture, larger lots in places, and a close-in location near central Brandon.

Valrico

Bloomingdale

Not one neighborhood but a broad area of many distinct subdivisions, known for mature oaks and established family homes. Addresses here can read Valrico, Brandon, or Riverview, so check the specific subdivision and its rules.

Valrico

River Hills

A gated golf community with distinctive homes and a more premium profile, often the next step for move-up buyers already living in the area. Club membership is separate from the home.

Valrico

Buckhorn

An established area of traditional single-family homes and mature landscaping, with golf-course surroundings in some sections and quick access to Bloomingdale and Lithia Pinecrest Road.

Buying an Established Home

The Details That Make an Established Home Worth Considering

Mature landscaping, established streets, and distinctive floor plans can make a resale home especially appealing. Understanding its condition, improvements, and ongoing costs matters just as much as finding the right neighborhood.

  1. Roof age. Many Florida insurers limit coverage on older roofs. Ask for the roof's age and permit, and get an insurance quote early.
  2. HVAC and water heater. Check the age and service history; replacement is a real cost to plan for.
  3. Insurance inspections. Older homes often need a 4-point inspection, and a wind mitigation report can lower premiums.
  4. Pool and screen enclosure. Have the pool equipment, surface, and enclosure inspected, not just the house.
  5. Renovation quality and permits. Confirm that major updates were permitted and done well, especially electrical and plumbing.
  6. HOA rules and flood information. Read the HOA's restrictions on fences, sheds, and rentals, and look up the flood zone for the address.

Buyer & Seller Questions

Brandon & Valrico: Common Questions

Both are unincorporated communities in Hillsborough County, so they share the same county government and public school district. Brandon is the more established and commercial of the two, built over several decades around the State Road 60 corridor, with quick access to I-75 and the Selmon Expressway. Valrico, to the east, is greener and more spread out, with mature oaks, larger homes in many neighborhoods, and golf communities such as River Hills. Brandon generally offers more convenience; Valrico generally offers more room.

It depends on what you want. In Brandon, Providence Lakes and Heather Lakes offer established single-family neighborhoods close to shopping, and the older Limona area offers mature trees and more varied homes. In Valrico, Bloomingdale is a broad area of established subdivisions known for its oaks, River Hills is a gated golf community popular with move-up buyers, and Buckhorn offers traditional homes with golf-course surroundings in some sections. Touring two or three of these on the same day is often the fastest way to narrow it down.

Start with the roof, since its age affects whether and how you can insure the home, then the HVAC system and water heater. Many insurers require a 4-point inspection on older homes, and a wind mitigation report can lower your premium. Have any pool, pool equipment, and screen enclosure inspected, confirm that major renovations were permitted, and look closely at the electrical panel and plumbing in homes built several decades ago. Look up the flood zone for the address as well.

Widely. Some older Valrico and Brandon streets have no HOA at all, many established subdivisions have modest HOAs with rules on fences, sheds, parking, and rentals, and gated golf communities such as River Hills have higher dues and more detailed restrictions, with club membership usually separate. Before making an offer, ask for the HOA's current dues, budget, and rules, and check the property tax bill for any community development district assessment.

Online estimates are a starting point, but they can't see your home's condition, updates, or lot.
